10.1.2 考场 120 分钟纸笔考前 5 分钟终极自查清单 (5-Minute Final Proofread Checklist)

在交卷前最后 5 分钟，停止继续重写段落，严格按以下 5 项顺序逐一扫卷，往往能直接挽回 3–5 分：

✅ 终极 5 分钟交卷防线（五步必查）
  1. 词数精准锁定 120–160 词：考场切忌逐字数浪费时间！数出 3 行的平均词数 × 总行数即可秒估。少于 115 词立刻对最薄弱的要点补 1 句原因或感受；超过 165 词勿再硬加废话，直接进入下一步排雷。
  2. 全篇时态一致性扫描：在 Ex 5 叙事段用笔尖圈出所有谓语动词，确认过去经历是否误跳成一般现在时（如 went ... and see），以及 Ex 6 Report 建议段是否准确使用 should be + 过去分词 / would enhance。
  3. 标点与逗号拼接 (Comma Splice) 清零：每看到一个逗号，立刻检查后半句是不是完整“主语+谓语”独立句！若是，马上加连词 so / and / yet 或把逗号改成分号/句号，或改写成 , which ...。
  4. 语域与体裁红线核对：确认 Ex 5 非正式邮件有自然缩略词（I'm, didn't, can't）、友好称呼与祝福；确认 Ex 6 正式 Report 零缩略词、零感叹号、零口语词 (kids / awesome / lots of)；Article 有吸睛标题与读者互动。
  5. 题号漏答与 Bullet Points 全覆盖核验：翻回第一页确认 Ex 1–4 每空/每道选择题均已填涂清晰（尤其 Ex 1 的 Give three ways 是否写满 3 行）；再用笔核对 Ex 5 的 3 条题目要点与 Ex 6 提示观点，确认无一遗漏！

📖 Exercise 1 · Short-Answer Reading Comprehension (8 marks · 建议用时 10 分钟)

Read the article about solar-powered boats on Lake Titicaca, and then answer the questions.

Solar-Powered Boats on Lake Titicaca

P1 Resting 3,812 metres above sea level between Peru and Bolivia, Lake Titicaca is the highest navigable lake on Earth. For decades, small timber tourist ferries carrying curious visitors out to its famous floating reed islands relied on roaring diesel engines. Not only did constant engine vibration frighten away wading birds from their shoreline nests, but leaking fuel also left oily slicks across the calm shallows, tarnishing the delicate reed beds local fishermen depended on for shelter, fishing traps, and woven crafts.

P2 Four years ago, a community cooperative led by marine engineer Mateo Quispe set out to replace those ageing ferries with silent, solar-powered catamarans. At the very start, raising modest funds from regional banks proved tough, yet the biggest early setback turned out to be persuading veteran boat captains that electric motors could handle sudden squalls. Many experienced skippers feared losing power mid-lake kilometres from the nearest jetty when dark storm clouds rolled swiftly off the surrounding Andes.

P3 Rather than importing costly fibreglass hulls from distant coastal shipyards, Quispe's workshop combined recycled aluminium frames with compressed layers of dried totora reeds—the same buoyant plant villagers have woven into floating islands for centuries. Roofing each vessel with lightweight solar panels cut overall hull weight by nearly a third while halving material expenses compared with conventional imported tourist boats.

P4 Operating solar craft at nearly four thousand metres introduced unexpected technical quirks. Although crisp mountain air delivered fierce sunshine, afternoon hailstorms regularly battered the panel coatings, and freezing overnight temperatures sharply reduced battery output at dawn. Engineers overcame both problems by fitting detachable clear hail-guards and wrapping the battery compartments in thick woollen felt salvaged from local weavers.

P5 To win over sceptical drivers, the cooperative ran six weeks of paired training voyages across gusty crosswinds before a single tourist stepped aboard. Seeing an electric boat glide steadily past a struggling diesel craft into a headwind convinced even the oldest skippers. Soon afterwards, the solar charging pier built for the fleet began supplying free evening electricity to the village health clinic and the island primary school, enabling children to finish homework without smoky kerosene lamps.

P6 Today, twelve solar ferries operate daily without buying a single litre of diesel. Because passengers hear wind rustling through the reeds rather than chugging motors, wildlife sightings along the tour route have risen noticeably, and local guides enjoy unhurried conversations with curious travellers. Buoyed by this success, the cooperative is currently designing a high-speed solar ambulance boat capable of reaching remote lakeside hamlets within twenty minutes during medical emergencies.

Read the descriptions of four teenagers (A–D) who started small local initiatives, and then answer Questions 1–9.

Four Teens Who Started Small Local Initiatives

A · Priya Living in a crowded tower block without private gardens, I mounted an old waterproof wooden letterbox in our ground-floor lobby and filled it with hand-labelled packets of basil and tomato seeds. At first, I worried hurried neighbours might see the shelf as pointless clutter. Thankfully, my retired grandfather—a keen grower for forty years—showed me exactly how to dry and store heirloom seeds so they actually sprouted. To keep costs at zero, I relied strictly on rescued jam jars and scrap timber from a carpenter's skip. Lately, older tenants who rarely chatted with younger families have started tucking handwritten recipe cards beside the packets! Before I leave for college in September, I am typing up a clear starter guide so incoming teens can keep the swap running smoothly.

B · Mateo Every Saturday morning, I wheel a folding repair stand outside our busy commuter train station to pump flat bike tyres and tighten loose brakes free of charge. I nearly gave up in week two when driving rain soaked my cardboard posters and just one shivering cyclist stopped by. Since nobody in my family knew mechanics, I taught myself everything through online tutorial clips, though I still spend a bit of pocket money each month on chain oil. Although I originally pictured fellow students rolling up before weekend football matches, most of my regular visitors turned out to be hospital shift nurses and local food couriers rushing between jobs. Sadly, strict council rules requiring expensive public-liability insurance have blocked my plan to set up a second stall in the town square.

C · Hannah Watching my elderly aunt struggle to book an online clinic appointment sparked the idea for my Thursday digital drop-in at Maplewood Retirement Home. For a whole month I put off knocking on the manager's door because speaking to strangers made me anxious, yet the staff welcomed me warmly. Early sessions hit a snag when ceiling lights glared across tablet screens, so I spent my savings printing large-font laminated cheat sheets for each resident. Rather than handing over finished tasks, I guide pensioners through making video calls and spotting fake text scams independently. Seeing their relief when a family call connects makes every Thursday worthwhile. Next term, I plan to ask my headteacher to recognise these afternoons as official school community-service hours.

D · Kwame Noticing friends struggling to focus in noisy flats or packed public libraries, I asked our local cricket club if I could open its unused pavilion as a quiet study café on weekday evenings. Initially, committee members doubted teenagers would respect the space and feared scratched tabletops, until I drew up a tidy-up checklist and deposit system that reassured them. We run a strict whisper-only policy with donated floor lamps and peppermint tea sold at cost price—a small fee I feared teens might reject, though thankfully they love the calm atmosphere. Recently, I trained two younger Year 10 pupils to handle keys and supplies so the café continues running reliably while I sit my summer exams.

🗒️ Exercise 3 · Note-Making (7 marks · 建议用时 10 分钟)

Read the article about urban bio-swales, and then complete the notes.

Urban Bio-Swales: Turning Streets into Sponges

P1 Modern cities are dominated by sealed tarmac roads and concrete pavements that shed rainwater almost instantly into underground drains. Whenever sudden tropical downpours or intense cloudbursts strike, those ageing subterranean pipes quickly overflow, flooding busy intersections, tram tracks, and ground-floor basements. In response, forward-thinking urban planners in cities ranging from Portland and Rotterdam to Melbourne are gradually replacing rigid concrete roadside gutters with bio-swales—shallow, sloped planted channels that resemble tidy street-side flowerbeds yet perform like carefully engineered living sponges along everyday neighbourhoods.

P2 Each bio-swale replaces solid vertical kerbs with strategic cut-out openings that guide rushing street water off the asphalt into deep engineered layers of coarse gravel, porous biochar, and hardy moisture-tolerant native sedges. First, these loose mineral layers absorb heavy rainfall rapidly, keeping excess stormwater out of overloaded drainage pipes during peak bursts. Second, dense plant roots and beneficial soil microbes filter out engine oil, brake particles, tyre dust, and trace heavy metals washed from busy traffic lanes before polluted runoff can discharge into nearby rivers and coastal bays.

P3 Early municipal trials in temperate and subtropical districts alike reveal that the practical benefits extend well beyond emergency flood control. On scorching summer afternoons, moisture steadily evaporating from dense street-level foliage cools surrounding asphalt and pavements by several degrees, softening dangerous urban heat islands around bus stops, clinics, and school gates. Municipal finance officers have also welcomed the audited figures: retrofitting vegetated swales along existing avenues typically costs roughly thirty per cent less than excavating wider reinforced-concrete storm tunnels underneath crowded underground utility cables and water mains. Neighbourhood shopkeepers even report that shadier, greener streets encourage pedestrians to linger longer outdoors, although long-term retail sales and property trends remain difficult to isolate statistically.

P4 Nonetheless, keeping bio-swales functioning smoothly year-round presents stubborn practical headaches for city maintenance teams. In regions experiencing icy winters, de-icing rock salt spread across snowy roads washes straight into the roadside channels and kills sensitive young shrubs, forcing landscape crews to replant damaged strips every spring. Furthermore, drifting fast-food wrappers, drinks cans, and damp autumn leaves frequently block the narrow kerb inlet gaps, sending pooled rainwater spilling back across pedestrian footpaths during severe storms. Finally, residents who mistake tall wild sedges for neglected weeds occasionally submit tidy-up complaints, while careless delivery drivers sometimes crush fragile plant borders while parking too close to the kerbside verge.

🎯 Exercise 4 · Multiple-Choice Reading (2024+ New Format · 6 marks · 建议用时 13 分钟)

Read the article about school repair cafés, and then answer Questions 1–6.

Should Every School Have a Repair Café?

P1 At first glance, a scarred wooden workbench piled with tangled earphones, split backpack straps, and a wobbly desk lamp looks oddly out of place inside a modern secondary school. Yet every Thursday after final lessons, twelve pupils at Oakridge High gather beside two retired neighbourhood electricians to bring broken everyday belongings back to life free of charge. Co-founder Amara Patel insists the club did not begin as an environmental protest; rather, students simply grew fed up watching tiny faults turn expensive gadgets into instant bin fodder.

P2 Supporters argue that school repair cafés teach hands-on problem-solving far more vividly than any science worksheet. When a teenager traces a loose solder joint on a tiny circuit board or reinforces a torn sports bag, they begin viewing familiar products as understandable mechanical systems rather than sealed magic boxes. Teachers also report an unexpected shift in classroom confidence: quiet pupils who hesitate to raise their hands during theory lessons frequently step up at the workbench, patiently walking younger classmates through each diagnostic step.

P3 Nevertheless, not every headteacher is rushing to hand teenagers soldering irons and screwdrivers. Critics raise understandable safety and legal objections, warning that tampering with mains-powered appliances could risk electric shocks or invalidate school insurance policies unless strict voltage limits apply. Oakridge High sidestepped this barrier by banning anything designed to plug directly into a wall socket—limiting weekly projects strictly to battery-powered devices, textiles, and bicycles. Yet sceptics counter that such cautious rules conveniently leave out kettles, toasters, and hairdryers: the very household appliances most frequently dumped in municipal landfill.

P4 Another subtle obstacle has less to do with safety regulations and far more with student psychology. Several schools that trialled monthly repair clubs watched attendance dwindle rapidly once the initial novelty wore off. Unlike ordering a cheap online replacement in seconds, tracking down an invisible fault demands slow trial and error, and some teenagers feel embarrassed when an attempted fix fails completely, quietly dropping out the following week. Oakridge's adult mentors soon realised that praising "honest diagnostics"—figuring out precisely why an object cannot be saved—matters just as much as celebrating triumphant repairs.

P5 Whether every secondary school timetable can squeeze in a permanent weekly repair bench remains an open question, particularly when teachers already juggle crowded examination schedules, tight budgets, and limited workshop storage space. Even so, as commercial manufacturers increasingly seal sleek phones and laptops shut with industrial glue against amateur screwdrivers, giving young people guided, structured opportunities to open everyday objects—safely, patiently, and collaboratively—may prove an indispensable educational way to raise resourceful citizens rather than passive, throwaway consumers.

✉️ Exercise 5 · Informal Email (15 marks · 建议用时 35 分钟)
Ex 5 · Official PromptContent: 6 + Language: 9 = 15 marks
Last weekend you went on a guided outdoor photography walk organised by a local youth club.
Write an email to your friend Alex about the experience. In your email, you should:
• describe where you went and what you took photos of
• explain one unexpected problem that happened during the walk
• suggest why Alex should join you at next month's session.

Write about 120 to 160 words.
You will receive up to 6 marks for the content of your email, and up to 9 marks for the language used.

提示：动笔前先列出 3 个 Bullet 各自要写的 1–2 个具体细节；确保语气亲切自然（可适当用缩略词与短语动词），严格控字在 135–155 词。

展开 Ex 5 Band 9 (15/15) 满分示范邮件 + 实测词数与评分批注
Band 9 Model Email · Python 实测词数：144 words (黄金区间 135–155 词) Content 6/6 · Language 9/9
Hi Alex,

How's your week going? You'll never guess what I got up to on Saturday. I joined our youth club's sunrise photography walk along the old canal towpath!

We followed a mossy stone footpath past abandoned brick locks, where pale mist hung low over the water. I managed to capture two herons swooping past wooden barges, along with bright reflections of autumn trees glowing against the brickwork.

Halfway round, though, a sudden downpour caught everyone off guard. Because I'd forgotten my waterproof pouch, my lens misted up completely, so I spent ten minutes drying it inside my jacket before snapping another shot!

Despite the soaking, our guide shared brilliant framing tricks I'd never tried before. Next month we're heading to the hilltop windmill at dusk. You should definitely come along since you've just got that new camera, and it's completely free!

Cheers,
Maya
  • Content 6/6 三条题目 Bullet 均给出生动画面与具体原因：① 地点（运河纤道与旧砖闸）+ 拍摄对象（苍鹭、木驳船、秋叶倒影）；② 突发问题（暴雨导致未带防水袋的镜头起雾及处理方式）；③ 邀约理由（下月黄昏风车主题 + 刚好试新相机 + 完全免费）。
  • Language 9/9 完美契合 Informal Email 语域：恰当融入缩略词（How's / You'll / I'd / we're / you've）、地道短语动词（got up to / caught off guard / come along）、非限制定从（where pale mist hung low...）及分词结构，通篇零语法失误、词数 144 词。

提示：Article 面向同校读者，宜用半正式且有感染力的语气（首段吸睛设问 + 双面辩证分析 + 结尾折中可行方案），切忌照抄题干原句，必须用自己的词伙转述！

展开 Ex 6 Band 9 (15/15) 满分示范文章 + 实测词数与评分批注
Band 9 Model Article · Python 实测词数：144 words (含标题；正文 137 words，均在 135–155 词) Content 6/6 · Language 9/9
Phones Off, Friends On? Rethinking Break-Time Rules

Have you noticed how eerily quiet our courtyard becomes at lunchtime, even when two hundred students sit side by side? With smartphones glued to almost every palm, proposals to ban phones entirely during breaks have sparked lively debate across school.

Admittedly, switching screens off offers clear rewards. Without constant alerts eroding our focus, teenagers would naturally spark real conversations, join courtyard sports, and return to afternoon lessons far less mentally drained.

However, a blanket ban overlooks practical needs. Many pupils rely on brief messages to arrange lifts home with parents, while societies frequently post urgent room updates online. Removing devices completely therefore risks causing avoidable stress.

Instead of strict prohibition, why not establish phone-free dining halls alongside one quiet courtyard zone where essential messaging remains allowed? Striking this sensible balance protects genuine friendship without sacrificing everyday convenience.
  • Content 6/6 完整回应校内争议并巧妙拓展两个提示观点：① 正方收益（避免通知分散注意、激发面对面交流、投入运动、减轻下午上课疲惫）；② 反方实际痛点（联系家长接送、查看社团临时教室变更）；③ 提出富有建设性的折中结论（无手机餐厅 + 允许紧急联络的指定静区）。
  • Language 9/9 典型 Band 9 校刊风格：韵律感标题、开篇与结尾两次设问互动、高分词伙（eroding our focus / mentally drained / blanket ban / striking this sensible balance），且全篇零照抄题干原句，含标题精准 144 词（正文 137 词）。
