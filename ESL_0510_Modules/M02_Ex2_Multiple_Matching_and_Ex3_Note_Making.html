2.1 Ex 2 多段配对 (9 分)：同义改写矩阵与“单遍横扫法”

Exercise 2 Multiple Matching (9 Marks): Paraphrase Anchors, Attitude Traps & One-Pass Scanning
Paper 1 Ex 2 · 9 Marks (Text A–D 配对) 建议用时 ≤ 12 min · 阅读最高分占比题

一、为什么“原词重现 = 90% 陷阱”？

Exercise 2 是 Paper 1 阅读部分分值最高的一道题（共 9 分），通常由一篇被划分为 A、B、C、D 四段（少数考卷为 A–E）的连续文章或四位人物独白组成，要求你把 9 个观点或经历精准配对回对应段落。

剑桥命题官最经典的出题手法是“原词诱饵 + 同义正选”：如果题干出现 budget / cost（预算/花销），Text B 里往往也会出现原词 cost（例如 "cost far less than I feared"），但真正谈“经济压力导致推进受阻”的 Text C 却通篇不写 cost，而是改写为 "hopelessly out of reach for my wallet" 或 "steep expenses"。只靠扫原词，十有八九踩中干扰项！

题干常见概括词 (Question Stem) 原文高频同义改写 (Paraphrase in Text) 必须核对的态度/极性陷阱
initial hesitation / doubted at first
起初犹豫、怀疑		 had second thoughts early on / wondered if it was pointless / felt reluctant initially 注意区分“别人怀疑我” vs “我本人起初动摇”。
financial obstacle / steep expenses
资金障碍、成本高昂		 struggled to cover the fees / out of reach for my wallet / pricey equipment 若前有否定词 "didn't cost a penny"，则与花钱困难相反！
unexpected benefit / unplanned bonus
意料之外的收获		 something I hadn't bargained for / turned out surprisingly rewarding / to my amazement 必须同时满足“事前未料到”与“结果积极”两层含义。
criticised the organisation / rules
批评组织或规定		 found instructions confusing / poorly coordinated / rigid tutor refused to let us... 看清批评主体是谁：组织方/指导员，而非抱怨天气或自己粗心。
mental relief / calming effect
舒缓情绪、减轻压力		 quieted my racing thoughts / helped me switch off from exam anxiety 不要和“体力疲惫（exhausting physically）”混为一谈。

未来打算或职业延伸		 setting up a weekend stall next year / hope to turn this pastime into a living 锁定未来时态（am going to / intend to），排除过去曾想做但已放弃的事。

二、“先读九题画标签 → 单遍横扫 A→B→C→D”高效做题法

很多考生习惯“读第 1 题 → 扫 A/B/C/D 四段；再读第 2 题 → 又扫 A/B/C/D 四段”，这相当于把整篇文章重复扫读 9 遍（共 36 次切换），极易超时且视觉疲劳。采用下述三步单遍算法，可直接省下 40% 时间：

  1. Step 1 · 90 秒读透全部 9 题并标“概念短码”：动笔读正文前，一口气通读 Q1–Q9，在每道题旁用中文或英文写 2 个字的语义核心（例如 Q1 初疑、Q2 打脸居民、Q3 缺钱找免费、Q4 错误指南）。
  2. Step 2 · 逐段精读 A → B → C → D，每段只读 1 遍收网！：精读 Text A 时，让脑中这 9 个标签同时待命；每读完 1–2 句就问自己“这句话满足哪个标签？”。通常读完 A 段能直接收割 2–3 题，立即填上 A 并划掉已解决的题号；接着用同样方法一次性扫完 B、C、D。
  3. Step 3 · 分布均衡检验 + 悬疑复核：填完 9 题后检查字母分布——在 4 段配对中，每个字母（A/B/C/D）几乎必定出现 2–3 次（绝对不会出现某个段落 0 次或 5 次！）。若某个字母只出现 0–1 次，优先回看该段重点核对未拿准的题号。

2.2 Ex 3 双标题笔记填空 (7 分)：拆骨留干与边界控制法

Exercise 3 Note-Making (7 Marks): Heading Scoping, Syntactic Trimming & One-Point-Per-Line Rule
Paper 1 Ex 3 · 7 Marks (2 大 Heading) 建议用时 ≤ 10 min · 严禁超行与串标题

一、利用两大 Heading 小标题“双向锁域”

Exercise 3 要求考生阅读一篇信息说明文（约 400–450 词），并在试卷给出的两大 Heading（小标题）下各填出 3–4 条要点笔记，共 7 分（例如 Heading 1 占 3 分 + Heading 2 占 4 分）。题型本质是考查“分类摘录 + 语言凝练”。

  • 第一步 · 先圈两大 Heading 边界词：常见组合包括 Advantages vs Disadvantages、Reasons for X vs How to solve X、Features of Y vs Advice for visitors。同一段落经常前半句讲优点、转折后半句讲困难——把“解决办法”写进“面临困难”标题下，即使抄得一字不差也是 0 分！
  • 第二步 · 检查是否已给 Example（示例要点）：部分考卷会在某一个 Heading 下提前印出一条示例（如 • Example: lowers carbon footprint）。务必第一时间回原文划掉该信息，绝不把同一个意思重新换种说法填进剩余空位！

二、“长句拆骨留干法”与一空一点铁律

Ex 3 笔记精炼公式（拆骨留干）：
[冗余主语/时间铺垫] + Core Verb + Core Noun Phrase + [未问到的 because 原因从句] → 只保留中间核心短语！
核心原则：1 条横线 = 1 个独立得分要点。写短语即可，不需要完整句号句子，保证不超出横线排版宽度！
考场常见失分错误 低效/错写示范 满分拆骨留干写法
1. 带入多余主语与修饰从句 在 Items to bring 下写：
Anyone joining the coastal walk must remember to pack a waterproof jacket because winds shift fast.		 (a) waterproof jacket
（直接砍掉主语、情态动词与未询问的 because 原因从句）
2. 把两个得分点全塞在同一行 第 1 空写：saves water and grows crops year-round
第 2 空：写不出新要点或重复		 第 1 空：saves water / uses less water
第 2 空：grows crops all year（分空放才拿满 2 分！）
3. 遗漏关键程度词/否定词 原文：reduces electricity use by 40% → 误抄成 electricity use 必须保留动作方向动词：reduces electricity use（否则不知是增加还是减少耗电！）
4. 串写到另一 Heading 领域 在 Difficulties faced by operators 下写 installing LED lights helps plants grow 时刻回看当前 Heading 属性：困难栏只能写 high cost of LED lights 这类负面制约因素。

Hands-On Heritage: Four Young Volunteers Share Their Restoration Projects

Text A · Maya (Urban Riverbank Rewilding)
I joined our city’s canal clean-up group straight after seeing a bright poster taped outside the public library. The safety guidelines were crystal clear from day one, and I never once worried about whether our small weekend team could make meaningful progress. Most local residents living above the towpath, however, insisted nothing green would ever survive beside a noisy six-lane ring road choked with delivery lorries. Imagine their shock when bright blue kingfishers began nesting among the reed beds we had planted barely six months earlier! What truly made the project unforgettable was spending every Sunday morning knee-deep in cool riverbank mud alongside seventy-year-old retirees. People from opposite generations who would normally never exchange two words in a supermarket queue quickly swapped flasks of ginger tea and stayed close friends long after the reeds grew tall.

Text B · Liam (Restoring a Historic Wooden Boat)
At first, I kept wondering whether scraping peeling paint off rotting oak planks every Saturday morning was a complete waste of my weekend. Everyone assumes classic boat restoration must cost a fortune, though a local maritime charity covered every single copper nail, brush, and timber board long before we arrived at the yard. Kept safely inside a dry harbour shed out of the winter rain, our small student crew soon settled into a steady, comforting rhythm. Slowly sanding curved wooden beams and brushing on smooth coats of warm varnish turned out to quieten my racing thoughts far better than flicking nervously through flashcards before school mock exams. By spring, seeing our century-old fishing hull slide smoothly back into the muddy estuary gave me a surge of quiet pride I had never felt over any classroom trophy.

Text C · Priya (Traditional Ceramic Tile Workshop)
I signed up for a six-week heritage tile course expecting to paint vibrant geometric patterns onto fresh clay straight away. Unfortunately, our master potter refused to let beginners sketch original motifs, insisting we press identical plain grey squares into wooden moulds for three solid weeks—a strict policy that nearly drove me right out the door! Worse still, commercial glazes and electric kiln hire looked hopelessly out of reach for my modest student wallet until a closing neighbourhood studio allowed us to haul away dozens of leftover clay buckets for nothing. Once I finally mastered the basic glaze chemistry and firing temperatures, everything clicked into place. As soon as my final school exams wrap up next June, I am teaming up with two classmates to launch a Saturday market stall selling custom-painted house-number tiles.

Text D · Kenji (Mapping Forgotten Mountain Footpaths)
Hiking into the misty hills behind my hometown to verify old stone pathways sounded effortless on paper when I volunteered in autumn. On my very first morning survey, I foolishly set off wearing thin canvas trainers and a light cotton hoodie without checking the mountain forecast, ending up soaked and shivering when freezing sleet blew across the exposed ridge. Yet the real headache came from the town council’s printed walking guide, which proved over a decade out of date—repeatedly steering eager ramblers straight into padlocked farm gates and waist-high thorny brambles. Armed with handheld GPS trackers, loppers, and thick leather gloves, my student crew eventually logged forty kilometres of clear public trails, replacing missing wooden posts so future families out for a Sunday walk would never suffer the same confusion.

Ex 3 Passage 1 · Vertical Farming in Converted Underground Tunnels

A Beneath busy London streets, wartime air-raid shelters and disused Victorian railway shafts once sat completely empty and silent for decades. Today, several of those curved concrete tunnels glow bright neon pink, packed floor-to-ceiling with tall metal trays of peppery salad leaves, crisp radishes, and fresh kitchen herbs. Known as subterranean vertical farming, this innovative method places stacked crop beds directly beneath urban restaurants and grocery warehouses, cutting out the refrigerated lorries that normally haul perishable leafy greens across entire countries before they even reach supermarket shelves.

B Operating thirty metres underground brings striking environmental rewards that traditional open fields struggle to match. Because crops grow inside sealed subterranean chambers instead of dusty outdoor soil, farms continuously capture and recirculate nutrient-rich mist around plant roots, using roughly seventy percent less water than conventional agriculture. Moreover, thick layers of surrounding London clay act like natural thermal insulation, shielding tender basil, mint, and coriander from blistering summer heatwaves and sudden winter frosts so growers can harvest reliable yields twelve months a year without spraying chemical pesticides.

C Despite enthusiastic media headlines, subterranean growers face tough practical and financial realities long before steady profits appear. Without a single ray of natural daylight reaching below the busy pavement, every single leaf depends entirely on energy-hungry LED panels that run up hefty electricity bills month after month. Fitting heavy-duty ventilation ducts, automated watering pumps, and towering steel racks inside narrow, curved brick tunnels also demands massive initial capital investment that few independent farmers can easily borrow from cautious commercial banks.

D Daily operations inside cramped underground tunnels test human patience and physical comfort too. Workers must descend slowly in narrow industrial service lifts and spend long shifts under humid artificial light without a glimpse of blue sky, making staff retention noticeably harder than on sunny countryside estates. Meanwhile, limited ceiling height strictly restricts which plants can thrive commercially: fast-growing microgreens and culinary herbs fit stacked shelves easily, whereas bulky root crops such as potatoes or towering wheat fields simply cannot be grown profitably underground.

E Even so, urban planners from Seoul to Paris are actively surveying unused metro spurs, abandoned tram depots, and concrete car parks as future neighbourhood food hubs. Supporters remain confident that ultra-fresh, zero-mile city harvests will eventually outweigh steep energy costs as clean solar and wind power continue growing cheaper worldwide.

Ex 3 Passage 2 · Protecting Dark-Sky Reserves from Light Pollution

A Just a single century ago, almost every child in a small town or rural village could step outside after evening supper and trace the sparkling milky band of our galaxy clearly across the night sky. Today, more than eighty percent of the world’s population lives beneath skies veiled in artificial glare—a pale orange haze created by poorly aimed streetlamps, towering glowing billboards, and illuminated glass office blocks whose lights never switch off even when desks sit empty.

B Observational astronomers were the first to sound the alarm over disappearing stars, yet wildlife ecologists have now proved that wasted night light damages living creatures far closer to home. Newly hatched sea turtles, programmed by evolution to crawl toward the brightest moonlit horizon over the open ocean, regularly turn inland toward dazzling coastal hotel floodlights and perish on busy seaside roads. Nocturnal moths and foraging bats, meanwhile, grow hopelessly disoriented around harsh white streetlamps, missing crucial feeding hours and failing to pollinate fragile night-blooming wildflowers. Humans suffer silently too: bright blue-tinted LED glow leaking through bedroom curtains tricks the human brain into suppressing essential sleep hormones, leaving city residents sluggish, tired, and irritable the next morning.

C Fortunately, establishing an official Dark-Sky Reserve does not mean plunging whole towns into pitch darkness or endangering late-shift pedestrians walking home at night. Across rural Wales, southern New Zealand, and northern Arizona, local councils have begun fitting simple downward-pointing metal shields onto existing streetlights so concentrated beams illuminate pavements safely where people actually walk rather than spilling wasted glare upward into passing clouds.

D Forward-thinking communities also replace harsh blue-white bulbs with warm amber lighting, which preserves human night vision while proving far less disruptive to sensitive insects and migrating birds. In busy commercial districts, shopkeepers sign voluntary pledges to turn off decorative neon signs and empty-car-park floodlights promptly after 11 pm, saving thousands in municipal power bills overnight. Along quiet residential pathways, motion-activated sensors dim lamps to a gentle low glow until a walker or cyclist actually approaches down the lane.

E Beyond safeguarding fragile nocturnal ecosystems and cutting wasted municipal electricity, accredited dark-sky towns frequently discover a welcome local economic reward: enthusiastic "astro-tourists" from crowded cities happily book off-season winter guesthouses and quiet rural cabins just to gaze at bright winter constellations without needing an expensive telescope.

方法 12均衡校验
Distribution Sanity Check (A–D)

统计 A、B、C、D 各自出现次数：正常范围是每段 2–3 次。若某段出现 0 次或 4+ 次，极可能有一道题误选了关键词诱饵！
笔记 13锁域铁律
Heading Scope Lock (Ex 3)
Ex 3 做笔记前第一秒必须用笔框出什么？
点击卡片揭晓答案
框出两个 Heading 标题属性（如 Benefits vs Difficulties / Harms vs Measures）。串到对面标题栏即使内容完全真实也判 0 分！
笔记 14拆骨留干
Verb + Noun Skeleton Only
Ex 3 摘抄长句时哪两类成分必须坚决剪掉？
点击卡片揭晓答案
① 剪掉 开头主语/情态动词套话（如 People should remember to...）；② 剪掉 未提问的 because/since 原因从句，留纯净动宾或名词短语。
笔记 15一空一点
One Independent Point Per Bullet Line
为什么不能在 Ex 3 的一条横线上用 and 连写两个并列优点？
点击卡片揭晓答案
每条横线最多只给 1 分！把两个独立得分点用 and 写在一行等于白白浪费掉另一行的 1 分，必须拆到不同横线上。
笔记 16防方向词丢失
Keep Polarity & Action Verbs
精简 Ex 3 短语时，为什么绝对不能把“动词/增减词”一并删光？
点击卡片揭晓答案
例如写优势时若只填 water 或 electricity bills，考官无法判断是省水还是耗水！必须保留 uses less water / cuts electricity bills。
